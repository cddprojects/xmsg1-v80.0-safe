Reading Job Advertisements for Flexible Roles

Last reviewed October 2026

You have read plenty of job ads. Flexible roles have their own habits, and these are the details that matter most.

Key takeaways

  • Look for hours, pay basis, work mode and employment type first.
  • Salary ranges and 'up to' amounts are not the same as guaranteed pay.
  • Note probation and notice period before you apply.
  • Check the employer on an official source.

Details that decide whether it fits

  • Hours: fixed shifts, a roster, or "flexible" with no numbers.
  • Work mode: "WFH" can mean fully home, or one day a week. Ask.
  • Employment type: employee, contract or freelance. It changes CPF, leave and tax.
  • Pay basis: hourly, monthly, per task or commission.

Watch the pay wording

"Up to" is a ceiling, not a promise. Commission-only pay depends entirely on results. A range on MyCareersFuture is a guide to what the employer expects to pay. Ask what typical earnings are for the role.

Small print worth a second look

  • Training bonds or clawback clauses.
  • Probation length and the notice period during it.
  • Exclusivity or outside-work restrictions if you have another job.
  • Who supplies equipment and who pays for internet or transport.

Check the employer

Confirm the company exists and is the one actually hiring. Prefer official channels such as MyCareersFuture or the company's careers page. Our glossary covers less common terms.

Common questions

What does 'up to' salary mean?

It is the top of the range, not a guaranteed amount. Ask what most people in the role are paid.

What is a probation period?

An initial trial period after you start, during which the notice period may differ. Check your contract.
