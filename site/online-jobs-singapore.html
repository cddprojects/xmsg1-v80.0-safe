How to get started

5 steps.

  1. 1

    Pick a skill to offer

    Teaching, writing, research, admin or customer support are all in demand online.

  2. 2

    Prepare your tools

    Reliable internet, a quiet space and the software the role needs.

  3. 3

    Look for an identifiable business

    You should be able to find who is paying you and how.

  4. 4

    Get the terms in writing

    Rate, hours, payment date and whether you are an employee or freelancer.

  5. 5

    Start small and review

    Take a trial period or small first batch of work, then decide.

Before you accept

Practical things to check.

Who pays you

You should know the business name and how you will be paid before you start.

Costs to start

You should not have to pay to begin work. If a fee is requested, pause and verify the business.

Status

Online roles can be employment or freelance. This changes CPF, leave and tax.

Trial periods

Ask whether the first tasks are paid and on what terms.

Questions to ask

  • Who is the business and how can I verify it?
  • Am I paid per hour, per task or per month?
  • When and how is payment made?
  • Is there any fee for me to start? (There should not be.)
  • Is this employment or freelance?
  • What happens if sessions are cancelled?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Paying a fee to get started
  • Working without knowing who pays you
  • Ignoring the tax on freelance income
  • Relying on chat-app messages instead of written terms
FAQ

Common questions.

Are online jobs real?

Many are. Online tutoring, moderation and support are established roles. Check the employer and the terms.

What is online work from home?

Work you do from home over the internet, such as tutoring, support or admin, for a set rate.

Do online jobs pay CPF?

Only if you are an employee under a contract of service. Freelancers manage their own contributions.
