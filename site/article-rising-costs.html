Cost of Living and Household Income: The Case for Flexible Work

Last reviewed October 2026

If every month feels tighter than the last, you are not imagining it, and you are not alone. Housing, groceries, transport and family costs weigh on many households here.

Key takeaways

  • Part-time, remote or freelance work are common ways to add income.
  • Compare take-home pay with extra costs like transport and childcare.
  • Ask how and when you are paid.
  • Check government support schemes if money is very tight.

Why a side income can help

You may not be able to change your rent, but you may be able to change your income. Part-time, remote or contract work can add to what you earn without demanding a rigid full-time schedule.

Choose the arrangement that suits your budget

  • Part-time: predictable hours, usually as an employee with a contract.
  • Remote or hybrid: fewer commuting hours and costs.
  • Freelance or contract: can pay more per hour but less predictable, and you handle your own CPF and tax.

Look at the whole picture

Compare the take-home pay against the extra costs: transport, meals out and childcare. Ask how pay is calculated and how often you are paid.

If money is very tight, also explore government support. SupportGoWhere (supportgowhere.gov.sg) lists schemes you may qualify for. Beware of anyone promising fast money for an upfront fee.

Common questions

How many hours would I need for a meaningful side income?

It depends on the rate and your target. Try our hours calculator.

Is a side income taxable?

Income from employment and freelance work is generally taxable and must be declared. Check IRAS.
