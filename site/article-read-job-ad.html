Basics

How to read a Singapore job ad like an insider

A job ad packs a lot into a few lines. Knowing what to look for saves time and avoids surprises.

Key takeaways

  • Look for hours, pay basis, work mode and employment type first.
  • Salary ranges and 'up to' amounts are not the same as guaranteed pay.
  • Note probation and notice period before you apply.
  • Check the employer on an official source.

Start with the basics

  • Employment type: permanent, contract, part-time, freelance or temp.
  • Work mode: on-site, hybrid, remote or work from home.
  • Hours: fixed, shifts or flexible.

Decode the pay

A salary range shows what the employer expects to pay, and where you land depends on experience. "Up to" figures are a ceiling, not a promise. Commission-based pay depends on results. Check whether pay is hourly, monthly or per project.

Terms worth noting

  • JD: job description, the duties and requirements.
  • Probation: an initial trial period.
  • Notice period: how long you must give or receive before leaving.
  • AWS: annual wage supplement, often a bonus month.

Check the employer

Confirm the company exists and is the one hiring. Prefer official channels such as MyCareersFuture or the company's careers page. See our glossary for more terms.

Common questions

What does 'up to' salary mean?

It is the top of the range, not a guaranteed amount. Ask what most people in the role are paid.

What is a probation period?

An initial trial period after you start, during which the notice period may differ. Check your contract.
