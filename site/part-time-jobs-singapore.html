Part time jobs in Singapore: roles, hours and what to check

Most people pick up a part time job around a day job, studies or caring duties. The useful questions are less about the rules and more about the practicalities: which shifts, how they are rostered, and whether the pay still works after transport and time.

How to get started

5 steps.

  1. 1

    Work out your real availability

    List the days and time slots you can commit to every week, including travel time. Be realistic: reliability matters more than maximum hours.

  2. 2

    Prepare a one-page CV

    Lead with reliability, availability and any customer-facing experience. Keep it to one page.

  3. 3

    Search trusted sources

    Browse MyCareersFuture and the careers pages of employers you know. Read the full listing, including hours and pay.

  4. 4

    Check the terms

    Confirm hours, pay rate, pay date, CPF and leave before you say yes. Ask for it in writing.

  5. 5

    Start and review

    After a few weeks, check the hours still suit you. Raise any issues early.

Before you accept

Practical things to check.

Rostering

Ask how far ahead shifts are released and how swaps work. School holidays, festive seasons and sales periods often mean extra or longer shifts.

Pay details

Confirm whether pay is hourly or monthly, the pay date, and how public holiday work is treated. Keep your payslips.

CPF and leave

You know the framework. The practical step is to confirm in writing that CPF is paid from your first month and how leave is pro-rated.

Your day job

Read your main contract for outside-work clauses before you commit. Work pass holders should check with MOM first.

Questions to ask

  • How many hours a week, and are they fixed or rostered?
  • What is the hourly or monthly rate, and when is it paid?
  • Is this an employee contract with CPF?
  • How much notice do I get for shift changes?
  • Is there a probation period and what is the notice period?
  • Who do I contact if my hours need to change?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Accepting without knowing the hours
  • Not asking whether pay is hourly or monthly
  • Overcommitting and burning out
  • Ignoring the terms in your main job's contract
FAQ

Common questions.

How many hours is a part time job in Singapore?

An employee contracted to work fewer than 35 hours a week is classed as part-time under Singapore's part-time employment rules.

Do part-time employees get leave?

Yes, generally on a pro-rated basis. Check the current rules on the MOM website.

Can I do a part time job and keep my full-time job?

Often yes, but read your full-time contract first. Some employers require approval for outside work.
