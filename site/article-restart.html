Returning to Work After a Career Break: A Practical Approach

Last reviewed October 2026

Gaps in a CV are common: caring for family, retrenchment, health, study or simply needing a rest. Employers see them more often than you might fear.

Key takeaways

  • Career gaps are common and easier to explain than people expect.
  • Keep the explanation short and honest.
  • Part-time or contract work can be a gentle restart.
  • Refresh your CV, references and skills.

Say it simply and honestly

One or two calm sentences are enough: what happened, what you learned or did during that time, and what you want next.

Restart small

Part-time or contract work can rebuild confidence, references and recent experience without the pressure of jumping straight back to full time.

Freshen your profile

  • Update your CV with recent skills, courses or volunteering.
  • Ask a former colleague for a short reference.
  • Look into SkillsFuture options to top up relevant skills.

Take the first step at a pace that feels right. There is no deadline on starting again.

See our job types guide for a gentle place to start.

Common questions

How long a gap is too long?

There is no fixed limit. Employers care more about what you can do now and how you explain the gap.

Should I take part-time work first?

Part-time or contract work can help rebuild confidence and recent experience, and many people use it as a first step.
